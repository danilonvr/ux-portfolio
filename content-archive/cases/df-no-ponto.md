###### Product Design

# Increasing Predictability and Reliability in Public Transport in the Federal District

##### Company

SEMOB-DF

##### Role

Product Designer

##### Duration

1 Month

##### Year

2026

### Context

DF no Ponto is a **transport app** created by the Government of the Federal District to **help residents plan and track their journeys**. Although it offers real-time information and service alerts, **users report low confidence** in timetable accuracy, **inconsistent navigation and limited accessibility**.

In a region where many people depend on multiple buses daily, **unreliable information increases stress, delays and uncertainty.** Over time, users rely more on personal experience than on the app, which weakens trust and reduces continued usage.

### Solution

I redesigned the core flows to **improve predictability, reduce cognitive effort and rebuild user trust**. Based on research, interviews, heuristic evaluation and usability testing, I prioritised high-impact issues and simplified search, route planning, line tracking and notifications.

The updated experience centralises key information, clarifies real-time status and introduces accessibility improvements, with the hypothesis that **these changes** **would drive higher task completion, stronger trust in route data and increased retention.**

### Expected Results

##### **Higher Retention**

Increase weekly retention by 15%, showing that users return to the app consistently.

##### **Primary Adoption**

Increase by 20% users who consider DF no Ponto their main transport app.

##### **Accessibility Impact**

30% increase in task completion among users who rely on accessibility features.

##### **Faster Planning**

We're expecting 60% of users able to plan a journey in under three minutes.

### Initial Analysis

A useful app was going unused. The reasons behind that were not visible from the outside. **Looking into real usage patterns, reviews and direct conversations** revealed that four problems kept surfacing across every source:

#### Pain Points

###### **Accessibility**

**The interface isn't clear**, increasing decision-making insecurity.

###### **Reliability**

**The timetables are imprecise**, affecting users predictability.

###### **Navigation**

**The app flow is inconsistent**, creating friction during actions.

###### **Usability**

The app **demands significant cognitive effort** during usage.

### Learning with users

**Four people** who rely on public transport daily were invited to talk about their routines. The goal was not to ask about the app directly, but to **understand their behaviors**.

The conversations showed that **users preferred to build their own strategies**: leaving their homes earlier, memorising schedules and relying on personal experience.

With those behaviors in mind, **four participants were observed** using the app to complete real tasks: finding a line, planning a route, saving it, and tracking a daily trip.

The frictions didn't take long to appear. Navigation broke expected flows, route suggestions ignored basic safety logic and performance issues interrupted tasks at critical moments.

### Turning insights into strategy

The research pointed to consistent patterns. With a clearer picture of what was breaking down, **the problems were reframed as questions**: how might the experience become more predictable? How might users feel more confident navigating the app?

Each question opened space for possible solutions. **Dozens of opportunities were mapped**, connecting directly to the pain points identified earlier.

[IMAGE 484x336 alt="" src=https://framerusercontent.com/images/b9gWUfmOFeDlJdSokn3XtRKOBo.png]

### Focusing on bigger impact

Not every opportunity could be addressed at once. A prioritisation matrix filtered what had real impact, and the selected initiatives were then organised into delivery cycles, **defining a clear sequence for what would actually be built**.

### Redesigning the starting point

The home screen offered little context and no clear starting point. The new design **brought the most important actions to the surface**, **making information faster to find and easier to act on**.

### A new way to track lines

**Key information tags were added** to each line, along with real-time position tracking and a full itinerary view. The goal was to **make line data easier to understand** and help users **make faster decisions.**

### Routes that fit the moment

The old version only showed the fastest route, ignoring what users actually needed at that moment. The new design **offers route options based on different priorities**: saving money, arriving faster, or avoiding crowded buses. Users choose what matters most for each trip.

### Features built for the full journey

Three new features were designed to support users during and after each trip: **live route tracking, trip ratings and problem reporting**. Together, they **improve the experience in real time** and **build a data foundation** for future improvements.

### Validating the new features

With the new interfaces defined, **usability tests were conducted with 13 participants** to validate if the solutions actually made sense in practice.

Testing early made it possible to **identify friction points before moving to the final UI**, adjusting what was not working in earlier stages.

[IMAGE 484x336 alt="" src=https://framerusercontent.com/images/ZYegm6LOjGTeVpX6LTSKCWPoE.png]

#### Highlights

###### **+80% Task Success Rate**

Most participants completed the tasks without critical errors.

###### **Average Time Under 60s**

Tasks were completed within the expected time.

###### **Low level of misclicks**

Users completed their tasks without friction or confusing.

###### **Improvements Identified**

Screens that caused hesitation were identified and corrected.

### Next Steps

###### **Conduct MoreUsability Tests**

To validate the new and updated features.

###### **Prioritise Remaining "Later" Activities**

To evolve and enhance the developed system.

###### **Validate Impact on Trust and Metrics**

To measure all the changes that were realised.

###### **Explore Community Data Integration**

To increase predictability with real-time user information.

### Lessons learned

###### **Prioritisation**

Keeping focus on what mattered most made it possible to deliver the project on time.

###### **Pilot Testing**

Running pilot tests before the real sessions reduced errors and improved the quality of the results.

###### **Innovation is not always necessary**

Following market patterns and focusing on usability corrections was enough to bring significant improvements.

###### **Team and business constraints matter**

Considering the team and company limitations was essential to deliver something realistic and viable.
